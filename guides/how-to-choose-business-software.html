Most failed software purchases do not fail because the product was bad. They fail because the buyer chose before agreeing on the problem, or because nobody planned adoption. This framework keeps a purchase anchored to outcomes.

1. Write the problem, not the feature list

Describe in one paragraph what is going wrong today and what "fixed" looks like in numbers: hours saved per week, errors reduced, revenue tracked. Features come later.

2. Separate must-haves from nice-to-haves

List no more than eight must-haves. Everything else is a tiebreaker. Long must-have lists push you toward expensive enterprise tiers you will not use.

3. Build a short list of three to five vendors

Use category guides, peer recommendations and reviews to narrow the field. Browse our categories for what to look for in each type of tool.

4. Score vendors on weighted criteria

Agree the weights before demos so that a slick presentation does not reset priorities. Typical criteria: fit to must-haves, ease of use, integrations, total cost, vendor stability, support quality and data portability. Our vendor scorecard does the maths.

5. Trial with real data and real users

Demos show the happy path. Insist on a trial or sandbox, import a sample of your own data and have the people who will use it daily try the tasks they do most.

6. Check references and reviews

Ask each finalist for two customer references of similar size and industry. Read recent reviews from multiple sources, and pay attention to complaints about support, billing and renewals — those reveal how a vendor behaves after the sale.

7. Negotiate the whole contract

  • Renewal caps: limit annual price increases in writing.
  • Term length: trade a longer term for a better rate only if you are confident in adoption.
  • Implementation: negotiate onboarding fees and success criteria.
  • Exit: confirm data export format and timing.

Model the full cost with our total cost of ownership calculator before you sign.

After the purchase

Assign an internal owner, set a 90-day adoption goal and review it. Then come back and write a review — your experience will help the next buyer.
